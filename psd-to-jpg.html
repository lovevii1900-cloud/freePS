When JPG beats PNG (and when it doesn't)

FormatBest forWatch out for
JPGPhotographs, email attachments, quick previews, smaller filesNo transparency; quality softens if re-saved many times
PNGTransparency, logos, icons, screenshots and crisp UI graphicsFiles can be large for photographs
WEBPModern websites that need smaller image filesSome older apps and workflows still prefer PNG or JPG

Rule of thumb: if the design has transparent areas you need to keep, use our PSD to PNG converter instead. If it's a photo-like image headed for email, chat or a quick client preview, JPG gives you the smallest file.

Why convert locally instead of uploading your PSD?

Many converter sites ask you to upload the PSD to their server. That may be fine for a public graphic, but it is a bad default for client work, unreleased product designs or anything under NDA. freePS runs in the browser: the PSD parsing and export happen in your device memory. You get converter convenience without handing the file to a third party.

Local conversion also means there is no queue and no file-size upload wait. Very large PSD files are still limited by your device memory, so a desktop browser is best for heavy files.

Tips for a clean JPG export

  • Flatten mentally first — JPG merges everything, so double-check layer visibility before exporting.
  • Watch transparency — transparent areas export as a solid background. Hide or fill them deliberately.
  • Keep the original PSD as your master file; JPG is a delivery format, not an editable original.
  • Export once — each re-save of a JPG loses a little quality. Generate fresh from the PSD when you need changes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert a PSD to JPG without Photoshop?
Open the PSD in freePS, set the visible layers, then export through File → Save → JPG.

Does JPG keep transparency?
No. Transparent areas become a solid background color. Need transparency? Use PNG export instead.

Is the converter really free?
Yes — free for personal and commercial use, with no registration.

Why is my JPG smaller than a PNG of the same design?
JPG uses lossy compression tuned for photographs, which shrinks files dramatically. The trade-off is no transparency and slight softening of razor-sharp edges and text.
